# Little Red {child}
A Family Board Books retelling of Little Red Riding Hood (after the Brothers Grimm and Charles Perrault).

Personalised parts: {child} (the hero), {mum} (sends the basket), {grandma}, {helper} (the woodcutter: Daddy, an uncle, a big brother).
The Wolf is always our own illustrated character, never a family member. Nobody is eaten: Grandma outsmarts the Wolf.

## Spread 1
At the edge of the Whispering Wood, in a cottage with a crooked chimney, lived {child}.
{child} had a cape as red as a ripe strawberry,
so everyone called {child} Little Red.

## Spread 2
One sunny morning, {mum} packed a basket:
warm honey buns, a jar of soup and a bunch of daisies.
"{grandma} has a sniffly nose," said {mum}.
"Take her this, straight down the path. No dawdling, and don't talk to strangers!"

## Spread 3
"Straight down the path!" said Little Red,
and off went the red cape, tip-tap, tip-tap, between the tall green trees.
The birds sang hello. The squirrels waved their tails.

## Spread 4
But behind a mossy log, two yellow eyes were watching.
"Good morning, little one," purred the Wolf, with a smile far too wide.
"Where are you going with that delicious-smelling basket?"

## Spread 5
Little Red forgot all about strangers.
"To {grandma}'s house, past the three tall pines!"
"How lovely," said the Wolf. "Why not pick her some bluebells?"
And while Little Red picked, the Wolf ran, swish!, the short way through the trees.

## Spread 6
Knock, knock. "Who's there?" called {grandma}.
"It's me, Little Red," squeaked the Wolf in his very best little voice.
But {grandma} was nobody's fool. She peeked through the window, spotted a long grey tail,
and hid in the wardrobe. She took her rolling pin with her.

## Spread 7
The Wolf crept in. He put on {grandma}'s spare nightcap
and her round reading glasses, and hopped into bed.
"Hee hee," he chuckled. "Lunch will be here any minute."

## Spread 8
Little Red skipped up the path with a fistful of bluebells.
"Hello, {grandma}! Oh... {grandma}, what big EARS you have!"
"All the better to hear you with," said a voice from the bed.

## Spread 9
"{grandma}, what big EYES you have!"
"All the better to see you with."
"And {grandma}... what big TEETH you have!"
"All the better to..."

## Spread 10
"STOP RIGHT THERE!"
The wardrobe burst open, and out came {grandma} with her rolling pin.
In through the door came {helper}, who had been chopping wood nearby
and had heard every word.

## Spread 11
The Wolf leapt out of bed so fast that the nightcap flew one way
and the glasses flew the other.
He ran out of the door, over the hill and far, far away,
and he was never seen in the Whispering Wood again.

## Spread 12
Then {grandma}, {helper} and Little Red sat down together
for honey buns and soup.
"Next time," said Little Red, "I'll stay on the path."
"And I'll keep my rolling pin handy," laughed {grandma}.

The End.
